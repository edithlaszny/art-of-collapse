Art of Collapse Overlap Diagram

Six collapses that are usually drawn as a queue. They are not a queue. Their events are ordered; their processes run concurrently; and one of the six is not a member of the series at all — it is the ground the other five stand on.

Notation — the grammar of an overlap diagram

  • FrameHeader reads overlap diagram [system] <name> [scope], following SysML’s pattern but spelled out: this is not an OMG diagram kind and the header should not imply that it is. Of the standard kinds it sits closest to a parametric diagram — roughly a par unrolled along a time axis, with fuzzy extents and an explicit substrate.
  • LaneOne modelled decay process. Lanes are not swimlanes — they carry no allocation and no ownership. Vertical order is the load-transfer order, nothing more.
  • ExtentThe pale gradient bar. The temporal extent of the process, deliberately soft at both ends: no crisp onset is being claimed. Its width is a consequence of the decay rate, not a free parameter.
  • HeatThe filled area. Instantaneous rate of decay. This is where overlap actually lives — two lanes hot at once means two registers are failing together, and the upper strip integrates it.
  • TraceThe thin line, integrity from 1 to 0. Continuous by assumption; the assumption is doing work and should be argued, not hidden.
  • TickThe vertical rule. The threshold event: zero duration, strictly ordered. This mark is the "collapse" in the narrow sense — everything else on the lane is the process that surrounds it.
  • SubstrateBelow the axis, drawn as remaining depth rather than as a lane. Not commensurable with the lanes and deliberately not drawn as though it were.
  • GhostInk withdrawn from lanes whose bearer no longer exists. Marks undefinedness, not a low value. A reader who takes ghosting for "almost gone" has misread the notation.
What the notation does not encode. No control flow, no message passing, no allocation, no conditionals. It cannot say why a threshold falls where it does. And the horizontal axis is in dimensionless phase units — writing years on it would be the one decision that turns an ontological instrument into a prophecy.

Preface · what this is about

Someone once wrote down five things in a row. The list has circulated for close to twenty years, quoted in full and rarely examined, because a numbered list is the most persuasive shape an idea can take: it looks like knowledge before anyone has checked whether it is.

This page is an attempt to check. The method is unusual enough to be worth naming: an attempt to draw it.

Drawing is a form of testing. A diagram is not an illustration of an argument made somewhere else — it is a commitment device, and a severe one, because a notation refuses to be vague in exactly the places where prose lets you off. Prose can set "and then" between two claims and leave the reader to supply the relation. A diagram has to pick a mark, and every mark carries a type. Sequence or overlap. State or event. Continuant or occurrent. There is no way to draw the sentence and stay uncommitted.

So the interesting part of this exercise was never the picture. It was the moment the drawing refused.

Notation after notation was tried and each failed in the same informative way — and that kind of failure is evidence. When nothing available will hold a description, the first suspicion should not be that the instruments are too crude. It should be that the description is mis-typed. It was. Two ontological categories had been sharing a single word, and a sixth item had been added that was not the same kind of thing as the first five at all. Repair those two faults and the diagram becomes easy to draw — the usual sign that the real work happened in the concepts and not in the drawing.

Collapse is a good test case for precisely this reason: nobody can run the experiment. There is no cohort, no control, no repetition; the events are singular and their observers are inside them. Where measurement is unavailable, conceptual hygiene is the only discipline left — which is to say that here ontology is not a preliminary to the empirical work. It is the work that is available.

Two things this therefore is not.

It is not a forecast. The horizontal axis is in dimensionless units and there is no honest conversion to years. Nothing here says when, or whether. What it says is what kind of thing each of six items is, what would have to be the case for each to be under way, and what could count as evidence either way — questions that come before prediction and are not helped by it.

And it is not detachment. There are two easy postures toward this material, prophecy and paralysis, and both are ways of not thinking. Formalisation is the third. It cannot say what will happen. It can say exactly what a person would be committing themselves to in claiming that it will — and declining to say more than that is a form of seriousness rather than a retreat from one.

The model behind the curves is a toy: six logistic decays with rates chosen for legibility. Its worth lies entirely in what it forbids, not in what it produces. A diagram that cannot be wrong is decoration.

Why none of the existing diagram kinds will hold this

01The events are ordered. The processes are not.

Calling these six things stages commits you to a partition of one interval, and a partition cannot overlap — that is what makes it a partition. So either the sequence is real and the overlap is an illusion, or the overlap is real and the sequence is something other than staging.

The second is true, and the confusion is a use–mention slip between two ontological categories. Each collapse names two things: a process of erosion, extended and fuzzy at both ends, and a process boundary, punctual and sharp. The boundaries are strictly ordered. The processes freely overlap. Nothing is paradoxical once you stop letting one word do both jobs.

02What is decaying, formally

The five originals are not defined by any material fact but by a doxastic one: each is the dissolution of a belief held in common. That gives the series its type-uniformity, and it also supplies a crisp definition of the boundary event.

Treat a shared expectation as a generically dependent continuant — a pattern that exists as long as it has at least one concretization in at least one bearer. Then erosion is the monotone loss of concretizations, and the collapse proper is the vanishing of the last one. Gradual process, punctual event, one object. (This is an extension of the standard BFO reading, not a citation of it: the orthodox range for a GDC is information artifacts, and collective belief is a harder case.)

03Why the series stops at five, and not arbitrarily

Read the cascade as load transfer rather than succession. Each failure moves a function to a bearer of smaller scale: settlement system → market → state → community → person. Every step is a real demotion down a ladder of collective bearers.

The ladder is finite and its last rung is the individual. Below the person there is no smaller collective bearer, so there is no sixth doxastic term — not because the author's imagination gave out, but because the scale argument closes. The five-term list is complete in the way a proof is complete.

04So the sixth is not sixth

Ecological collapse has no doxastic component whatever. The biosphere holds no expectations and can lose none. Appended to the list as a sixth item it breaks the type-uniformity that made the list an argument, and the list quietly becomes a mood.

Kept, but reassigned, it becomes the strongest element in the picture. It is not the last term of the series; it is the bearer condition for every term — the material basis without which there is no population to hold or lose a belief. It runs beneath the sequence rather than after it, it starts earlier than any of them, and it is not driven by their cascade. In the model that follows this is not a rhetorical claim but a structural one: the substrate has its own parameter and is not coupled to the others.

05What the terminal state can and cannot show

Cross the substrate threshold and the five upper registers do not complete. They stop having truth conditions. There is no bearer, so there is no belief to be in any state, and "collapsed" is as wrong as "intact."

The diagram therefore has to depict its own failure of reference, and the honest rendering of an undefined value is not a value. Ghosting in this notation is not a low reading — it is the absence of a reading. What survives past that line is not the system's end state but the modeller's sheet of paper.
